Indian driving deserves direct study.

A dataset for understanding how vehicles see and move through unstructured urban scenes.

A two-wheeler moves alongside a car. An auto-rickshaw enters the junction. Traffic negotiates space on a road where lane markings explain only part of what happens next. For anyone who has driven in Bengaluru, these interactions are familiar. For a model learning to understand driving, they are a demanding part of the world to represent.

The dataset brings together recorded camera video, timing and calibration tables, privacy-transformed GNSS/pose information, and sampled semantic and depth outputs. The aim is to give researchers a practical starting point for studying Indian traffic with data collected in that setting.

The recordings include edge cases such as two-wheelers filtering between auto-rickshaws and a bus on wet roads, successive merges near construction barriers, and oncoming riders sharing narrow streets with queued and parked vehicles. A side camera can reveal an approaching rider before the rider enters the front view. These Bengaluru scenes complement global driving datasets and give researchers another setting for evaluating model generalization.

The main release contains approximately 4.006 TB in familiar MP4 and Parquet formats. Each recording has a stable recording_id connecting its different layers. You can explore a recording, choose the streams and time windows your experiment needs, and construct a task-specific subset without treating the entire collection as one download.

The audience includes researchers in autonomous driving, robotics, computer vision, video representation learning and data infrastructure. INDUS-AD provides observations and derived signals; researchers choose their own targets, filtering, synchronization and evaluation partitions.

More than a view
through the windshield.

A front camera tells only part of the story. A rider approaches from the side, a vehicle closes from behind, and a turn changes which view matters next.

Six surround views, plus a tilted front view

Front, front-left, front-right, rear, rear-left and rear-right video are present in all 1,469 main-release recordings. The additional tilted-front stream is available in 1,113 recordings; its absence in the remaining 356 is represented as missing data.

Multiple cameras provide complementary views of the same driving interval, supporting experiments that use the scene around the vehicle.

One regional setting, explored in depth

The recordings cover Bengaluru and its surrounding road network. They offer a setting for studying mixed traffic, road-user interactions, changing scene context and the practical difficulties of working with real sensor recordings.

Repeated routes and neighboring clips can share context. Evaluation partitions should account for recording identity, temporal adjacency and scene or trajectory overlap.

Video, motion and scene signals.
Connected by recording.

Start from the layer your question needs, then join deliberately. File counts, message counts and sampled labels describe different units.

Video and normalized sensor tables

The main release contains 9,927 MP4 files and matching camera-frame tables, plus 1,469 GNSS recording tables. Camera timing, calibration, motion, orientation, validity and sensor-quality fields make it possible to inspect the relationships behind a clip.

Normalized GNSS tables include channel identity, motion and sensor-quality fields. See the sensor reference for stream-specific analysis.

Sampled semantic and depth outputs

The 34,306 sampled camera-annotation rows include semantic class fractions, depth summaries, weather scores and image-quality features. Dense indexes connect these samples to semantic masks and depth maps in TAR archives.

Browse semantic and depth previews in the Viewer, or use the indexed archives for full-resolution outputs.

Sampled, model-generated annotations

Semantic, depth and weather outputs are sparse pseudo-labels rather than human-verified ground truth. Coverage varies by camera and recording; check the published coverage guide when selecting supervision.

Documented sensor geometry and timing

The reference documents define camera mounting coordinates, optical projection parameters, coordinate frames, units and transform direction.

Camera and GNSS timing policies, calibration conventions and missing-data handling are documented alongside the release.

Different questions.
A shared starting point.

Build task-specific experiments across video learning, motion, perception and robotics data infrastructure.

Video and world models

Explore temporal representations, future visual prediction and self-supervised learning from driving sequences. Multiple views support questions about relationships across cameras, while real traffic supplies changing interactions and scene context. Choose clip duration and sampling frequency to match the hypothesis.

Pose-derived trajectory research

Use video and valid pose histories to investigate future-motion targets or trajectory-oriented imitation learning. Document timing, coordinate frames, gap handling and target construction.

Perception and weak supervision

Study multi-camera context, sparse semantic/depth outputs and ways to select useful frames from longer recordings. Evaluate pseudo-label quality for your chosen task.

Localization and data tooling

Investigate GNSS/camera association, missing streams, timing gaps, quality filters and reproducible data selection. Preserve channel identity and validity flags.

INDUS-AD

Multi-camera video, GNSS and derived perception layers

The main camera/GNSS release: 1,469 recordings, 9,927 MP4s, 85.31 timeline hours and approximately 4.006 TB. Choose it for surround-view experiments and access to the wider set of normalized tables, sampled labels and dense references.

Six surround cameras are available across the release, with an additional tilted-front stream in a subset. It contains no LiDAR payloads. Access requests are reviewed manually.

Explore INDUS-AD

INDUS-AD Monocam

A separate single-camera release

Monocam contains 96 front-camera MP4s and 105,692 decoded frames, with normalized sensor tables, 10,608 sampled frame-label rows and semantic/depth archives. The decoded release is approximately 36.02 GB, including 22.85 GB of video.

A smaller entry point for monocular perception and video workflows. Download selected MP4s for local playback if your browser does not support HEVC.

Explore Monocam · See Monocam analysis

INDUS-AD 3D

The decoded LiDAR companion

The 3D release provides 76,052 indexed LiDAR scans for eight recordings shared with the main dataset, distributed across 31 payload TAR shards with recording, scan and calibration indexes. Join by exact recording_id, then assess timing for your task.

Use the main release alongside this LiDAR companion for camera and GNSS data. The current devkit supports the camera/GNSS workflow; LiDAR access uses the companion’s scan indexes and archives.

Scale when the workflow works.

A multi-terabyte release does not require a multi-terabyte first experiment.

  1. Pick the release and review access

    Choose a dataset card, review its license and request access where required.

  2. Inspect the layers separately

    Browse recordings, camera annotations, GNSS and semantic/depth previews in the Viewer, or work directly with the published files.

  3. Pin a revision and download selectively

    Choose a recording, pin its dataset revision and download the videos, tables or dense layers your experiment needs.

  4. Define and validate your experiment

    Set your sampling and evaluation policy, inspect the selected output, then expand to a larger subset.

From recordings to reproducible subsets.

The INDUS-AD devkit selects frames and scenes, interpolates GNSS poses, applies filters and scenario quotas, and exports validated train/test subsets with provenance in a nuScenes-compatible structure.

Quickstart code

Use Python 3.12 on Linux or macOS and sign in with a Hugging Face account approved for INDUS-AD. The example downloads about 209 MB of dataset files and needs about 2 GiB of working space plus the Python environment.

Bash
git clone https://github.com/gagandeepreehal/indus-ad-devkit.git
cd indus-ad-devkit
python3.12 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
python -m pip install -e .
hf auth login
cp examples/indus_ad_v21_config.json dataset_config.json
cp examples/indus_ad_annotations.jsonl annotations.jsonl
indus-ad build --config dataset_config.json
indus-ad validate --dataroot var/indus-ad-datasets/v1.0-trainval --version v1.0-trainval
indus-ad inspect --dataroot var/indus-ad-datasets/v1.0-trainval --version v1.0-trainval

Licensing and access

Published dataset releases use CC BY-NC 4.0 for non-commercial use, subject to their individual access requirements. Attribute the source and identify the release used. For raw recordings or commercial use, contact the author about a separate license.

Privacy transformations and residual risk

Published videos use automated face and plate blur, and GNSS publication reduces global position precision while retaining local motion geometry. These transformations do not guarantee anonymity. Do not re-identify or track people or expose sensitive locations.

Report concerns privately

Send privacy or takedown concerns privately to indus-ad@gagandeepreehal.com, including the recording ID or artifact path when known.

Evaluation and deployment limits

Researchers define their own train, validation and test partitions; Viewer split names are publication labels. The releases are intended for research, not validated production driving or safety decisions.
